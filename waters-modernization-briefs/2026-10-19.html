GIS Is an Enterprise Service, Not a Map Project

Published 19 October 2026 · 08:11 MDT

Modernization succeeds when geospatial capability is governed as shared information infrastructure—connected to authoritative records, business workflows, public services, and measurable operational outcomes.

Define the service

Identify the decisions GIS supports, the customers it serves, the authoritative layers it depends on, and the operational outcomes it must improve before defining tools or deliverables.

Assign authority

Every dataset and service needs a steward, publication authority, update cadence, quality threshold, access classification, retention rule, and support owner.

Minimum enterprise GIS scope

  • Authoritative-data register covering ownership, lineage, accuracy, currency, and approved use.
  • Documented integration contracts linking spatial services to records, cases, workflows, portals, and analytics.
  • Service-level expectations for availability, performance, recovery, monitoring, and support.
  • Security, privacy, records, accessibility, and public-disclosure requirements applied throughout the lifecycle.
  • Interoperable formats, documented APIs, configuration portability, and a practical exit strategy.
  • Training, operating procedures, and measurable adoption outcomes for business and field users.

Contract for outcomes

  1. Establish the current-state inventory and service baseline.
  2. Define deliverables with owners, dependencies, acceptance criteria, and evidence.
  3. Validate priority workflows and authoritative layers with business representatives.
  4. Pilot integrations and operational support using approved data.
  5. Accept each phase only after technical, business, security, records, and accessibility gates are satisfied.

Executive takeaway

A map is an output. Enterprise GIS is a governed service that makes location intelligence dependable across the organization.
