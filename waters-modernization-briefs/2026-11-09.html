Every Service Needs an Acceptance Model

Published 9 November 2026 · 08:17 MST

Modernization services create value only when expected outcomes, evidence, decision rights, and completion conditions are clear enough to test and accept.

Define the outcome

Describe the business capability, operational improvement, decision support, or risk reduction the service must produce—not merely the activities it will perform.

Assign acceptance authority

Each deliverable needs one accountable owner, named reviewers, objective criteria, required evidence, and a documented approval or exception decision.

Minimum acceptance model

  • Outcome, scope boundary, dependencies, assumptions, and accountable owner.
  • Deliverable definition with measurable functional and nonfunctional criteria.
  • Required artifacts, demonstrations, tests, operating procedures, and knowledge transfer.
  • Security, privacy, records, accessibility, architecture, and support evidence.
  • Review window, defect treatment, exception authority, and rework expectations.
  • Written acceptance showing what was approved, by whom, and on what evidence.

Govern the service

  1. Agree on deliverables and acceptance criteria before work begins.
  2. Link effort to milestones, evidence, and business outcomes.
  3. Review progress through completed evidence rather than activity reports alone.
  4. Record scope changes, exceptions, residual risk, and approval authority.
  5. Close only after operational ownership and knowledge transfer are complete.

Executive takeaway

A service without an acceptance model can consume time without proving value. Clear outcomes and evidence protect both the organization and the service provider.
