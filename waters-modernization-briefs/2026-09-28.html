Public-safe modernization case study

From Scanning Project to Governed Record System

Published 28 September 2026 · 08:41 MDT

Digitization creates images. Modernization creates trustworthy, searchable, governed records that remain usable across cases, maps, workflows, and generations of public servants.

Start with the record

Define the authoritative record, responsible steward, legal status, retention rule, access class, metadata, and relationships before selecting a platform or beginning mass migration.

Design for the lifecycle

Capture, quality review, classification, legal hold, discovery, amendment, disclosure, retention, disposition, and transfer must form one controlled lifecycle rather than disconnected scanning steps.

Minimum modernization controls

  • Documented provenance, chain of custody, immutable identifiers, and version history.
  • Representative OCR and classification testing with measurable accuracy thresholds.
  • Human review for low-confidence or legally significant records.
  • Role-based access, audit trails, retention schedules, legal holds, and defensible disposition.
  • Open export formats, documented APIs, and an exit strategy that prevents vendor lock-in.
  • Accessible search and retrieval that connects records to cases, locations, workflows, and authoritative datasets.

Recommended sequence

  1. Inventory record series, volumes, formats, custodians, restrictions, and authoritative systems.
  2. Define the records model, metadata standard, identifiers, quality criteria, and retention mappings.
  3. Run a controlled pilot using public, synthetic, or otherwise approved material.
  4. Score platform options against lifecycle, integration, security, accessibility, export, and total-cost requirements.
  5. Scale only after business acceptance, records approval, and independently verified migration controls.

Executive takeaway

A scanner can reduce paper. Only governance can preserve evidence. Treat records modernization as an information-governance program supported by technology—not as a hardware purchase or file-conversion exercise.
