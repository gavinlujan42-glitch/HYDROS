Governed Digital Water Modernization

Published 14 September 2026 · 08:54 MDT

A practical pattern for modernizing public water information while protecting authoritative records, human accountability, accessibility, and public trust.

Modernization focus

Build the foundation first: authoritative data, document intelligence, spatial context, interoperable services, identity-aware access, and complete auditability.

Governance focus

Require clear product ownership, decision rights, measurable acceptance criteria, records controls, accessibility review, security review, and transparent change management.

Recommended delivery sequence

  1. Inventory applications, data, documents, interfaces, workflows, and authoritative sources.
  2. Define a layered target architecture separating records, data, GIS, workflow, integration, search, and user experience.
  3. Translate discovery into a traceable, phased backlog with owners and acceptance tests.
  4. Validate candidate capabilities with public or synthetic data before production use.
  5. Advance through documented architecture, privacy, records, accessibility, cybersecurity, procurement, and business-acceptance gates.

Public-interest safeguards

  • AI assists; accountable people decide.
  • Every result retains provenance and links to authoritative material.
  • Access follows identity, role, and lawful purpose.
  • Automated classification and OCR are measured against representative test sets and reviewed when confidence is insufficient.
  • Public prototypes are clearly distinguished from official systems, filings, determinations, and legal advice.

Executive takeaway

Successful water modernization is not a single platform purchase. It is a governed operating model that joins trustworthy records, spatial intelligence, interoperable services, and human authority into an incremental delivery program.
