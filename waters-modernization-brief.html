Public-safe modernization case study

Operational Ownership Starts Before Go-Live

Published 23 November 2026 · 08:55 MST

A modernized public service is not ready when the software merely works. It is ready when accountable owners can operate, support, recover, govern, and improve it throughout its life.

Assign the service

Name both a business service owner and a technical service owner. Define who accepts outcomes, controls priorities, manages service health, and authorizes change.

Design for operations

Monitoring, support tiers, incident response, recovery, records, accessibility, training, and lifecycle management are design requirements—not post-launch tasks.

Minimum operating model

  • Business and technical ownership with clear decision and escalation authority.
  • Service measures, monitoring, alerting, support tiers, and response targets.
  • Incident, problem, change, release, and configuration-management practices.
  • Backup, recovery, continuity, capacity, and availability expectations.
  • Data stewardship plus records, privacy, security, and accessibility responsibilities.
  • Training, runbooks, knowledge transfer, vendor handoffs, and lifecycle funding.

Make readiness testable

  1. Inventory current services, dependencies, interfaces, and operational owners.
  2. Approve an ownership and support matrix before committing to deployment.
  3. Set measurable service targets and evidence requirements.
  4. Exercise observability, runbooks, escalation, recovery, and continuity procedures.
  5. Accept the service only after operations, business, and control owners sign off.

Executive takeaway

Go-live without operational ownership is not modernization; it is a transfer of unresolved risk. Treat supportability and stewardship as acceptance criteria from the beginning.
