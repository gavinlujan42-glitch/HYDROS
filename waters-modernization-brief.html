Public-safe modernization case study

Requirements Before Platforms

Published 5 October 2026 · 08:16 MDT

Technology selection should follow a clear definition of the public service, authoritative information, decision rights, controls, and measurable acceptance outcomes.

Begin with the mission

Document the service journey, business authority, authoritative sources, users, accessibility obligations, records lifecycle, and outcomes before comparing products or architectures.

Make acceptance testable

Every capability should have an owner, evidence source, acceptance criterion, control requirement, and traceable decision before it becomes committed scope.

Minimum discovery package

  • Current-state inventory of applications, data, records, maps, interfaces, workflows, and manual workarounds.
  • Authoritative-source register identifying stewardship, quality, retention, access, and disclosure requirements.
  • Priority service journeys and pain points validated by business representatives and end users.
  • Nonfunctional requirements for security, privacy, accessibility, resilience, interoperability, auditability, and export.
  • Traceability linking needs, requirements, controls, tests, approvals, and releases.

Use evidence gates

  1. Approve the problem statement and bounded discovery scope.
  2. Validate the current state and measurable target outcomes.
  3. Test critical assumptions with public, synthetic, or otherwise approved material.
  4. Score alternatives against the same weighted requirements and lifecycle costs.
  5. Advance only when accountable business, records, security, accessibility, and architecture authorities accept the evidence.

Executive takeaway

A platform can accelerate a sound operating model, but it cannot create one. Requirements, decision rights, traceability, and acceptance evidence are the foundation of durable modernization.
