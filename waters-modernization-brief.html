Public-safe modernization case study

Policy Rules Are System Requirements

Published 16 November 2026 · 08:24 MST

Public-sector systems implement authority, eligibility, evidence, exceptions, and due process. Modernization must therefore translate policy and legal rules into traceable, testable, and reviewable system behavior.

Capture the authority

Each rule should identify its authoritative source, responsible interpreter, effective period, affected services, required evidence, and conditions under which exceptions or review apply.

Separate policy from code

Rules change. Keeping them explicit, versioned, and linked to decisions and tests reduces hidden logic, inconsistent interpretation, and expensive redevelopment.

Minimum policy-to-system traceability

  • Authoritative source, citation, effective date, jurisdiction, and accountable steward.
  • Business rule, eligibility condition, calculation, evidence requirement, and exception path.
  • Affected data, records, spatial information, workflows, notices, and public services.
  • Privacy, security, accessibility, retention, disclosure, and audit obligations.
  • Test cases covering normal, boundary, exception, appeal, and historical scenarios.
  • Decision record showing interpretation, approval authority, assumptions, and review triggers.

Govern rule changes

  1. Maintain a versioned authoritative-rule register.
  2. Link requirements and tests to the governing rule.
  3. Require multidisciplinary review before implementation.
  4. Assess downstream data, workflow, notice, and reporting impacts.
  5. Preserve prior versions for audit and historical determinations.

Executive takeaway

When policy logic is undocumented, software silently becomes the policy. Traceability keeps authority visible, change manageable, and decisions defensible.
