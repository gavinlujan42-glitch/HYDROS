Dependency Maps Prevent Surprise Delivery Failures

Published 26 October 2026 · 08:07 MDT

Modernization plans fail when critical approvals, data, integrations, controls, resources, and acceptance authorities remain invisible until delivery is already underway.

Map more than technology

A useful dependency model connects business outcomes, authoritative information, services, interfaces, policies, approvals, people, contracts, operating capacity, and external obligations.

Expose the critical path

Each dependency should have an owner, required-by date, evidence of readiness, downstream consumers, escalation path, and consequence if it is late or incomplete.

Minimum dependency register

  • Business decisions and acceptance authorities required before work can advance.
  • Authoritative datasets, records, spatial layers, quality thresholds, and stewardship assignments.
  • Interfaces, identity services, shared platforms, environments, and operational-support commitments.
  • Security, privacy, records, accessibility, legal, architecture, and procurement review gates.
  • Staffing, funding, contracts, facilities, licensing, and external approvals where applicable.
  • Assumptions, sequencing constraints, status evidence, contingency actions, and review dates.

Govern the dependency chain

  1. Build the dependency register during current-state discovery.
  2. Link each item to requirements, milestones, decisions, controls, tests, and releases.
  3. Review high-impact dependencies at every governance gate.
  4. Prevent dependent work from being declared complete without evidence.
  5. Retire, replace, or escalate assumptions as facts become available.

Executive takeaway

A roadmap shows intended movement. A dependency map shows whether movement is possible. Governing both is what makes modernization executable.
