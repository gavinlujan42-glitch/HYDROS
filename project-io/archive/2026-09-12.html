Water · power · governance edition

Fallbacks need ledgers before emergencies.

Published September 12, 2026 · 8:09 AM MDT

ORANGE · CONTINGENCY WATCH

Oracle acknowledged grid power as a fallback if Project Jupiter’s on-site air permit fails. The primary plan remains a natural-gas-fed Bloom microgrid, but the alternative would change the project’s infrastructure boundary and could trigger county-agreement review.

Verified public record

At the September 8 county meeting, Oracle described working with a grid provider as a possible alternative if the air permit is not approved. County Chair Manuel Sanchez said the IRB and community-benefit agreements were structured around on-site power and avoiding demand on El Paso Electric, and a departure could require amendments.

The project’s current public site still says it will bear all energy costs, protect grid stability and fully power the campus with Bloom fuel cells. No utility selection, interconnection application, load study or rate-impact filing was located.

Oracle also said the $50 million state-linked commitment for county water infrastructure had been fully funded and was expected to move to Doña Ana County. No new public transfer, receipt or project-allocation ledger was found.

Unverified outcomes

Oracle says any grid fallback should benefit existing customers. Critics predict cost and reliability exposure. Those are competing positions, not demonstrated outcomes.

No new OSE meter history, August 31 acre-feet report, pipeline approval, air-permit decision or Supreme Court merits order was located. No evidence reviewed establishes corruption.

HYDROS judgment

A resilient decision process should evaluate both power branches now. Grid interconnection could alter generation-water assumptions, transmission land needs, emissions accounting and agreement obligations. The same provenance rule applies to the water funding: “fully funded” should resolve into appropriation, transfer, receipt, encumbrance, project and expenditure records.

Required evidence

  1. Grid interconnection and load-study records, including cost allocation.
  2. Agreement clauses triggered by a change from on-site generation.
  3. State transfer and county receipt records for the $50 million commitment.
  4. Project-level allocation of water and wastewater improvements.
  5. OSE meter readings and separate construction, initial-fill and operational ledgers.
