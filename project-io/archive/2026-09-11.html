Water, data and governance edition

Provenance is infrastructure.

Published September 11, 2026 · 8:03 AM MDT

ORANGE · DATA-QUALITY WATCH

Jupiter’s newest material disclosure concerns workforce data, but the control lesson applies directly to water: reported numbers require a source system, data dictionary, measurement window, validation rules and correction history.

Verified public development

New reporting based on an August 18 email says 189 Doña Ana County workers were identified while 1,600 workforce records could not be matched to ZIP codes. Oracle later reported 466 county residents and 3,686 construction workers on September 8.

STACK says workforce information is reported quarterly to the Department of Workforce Solutions; DWS reportedly said on August 19 that no responsive records existed. The timing and custody of the reports remain unresolved.

No new public OSE meter ledger, Oracle August 31 acre-feet report, pipeline approval, air-permit decision or Supreme Court merits ruling was located.

Water-governance implication

The workforce snapshots may be reconcilable through later hiring or data cleanup. The same discipline should govern construction-water claims: preserve raw meter readings, identify each well and customer, publish conversion methods, distinguish permitted capacity from actual pumping, and retain corrections.

Future environmental dashboards should expose provenance and quality controls, not only headline totals. The September 22 monitoring committee should request machine-readable extracts and reconciliation tables.

No evidence reviewed today establishes corruption or falsification.
